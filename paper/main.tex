\begin{abstract}
Natural Language to SQL (NL2SQL) stands poised to become the foundational execution substrate of conversational and voice-driven user interfaces over enterprise-scale relational data. Yet a rigorous gap separates current academic leaderboard pipelines on benchmarks such as BIRD-SQL from interactive, voice-ready deployment: monolithic single-pass prompts collapse under schema overload and dirty cell literals, whereas brute-force multi-agent ensembles achieve high Execution Accuracy (EX) only by sampling dozens of homogeneous completions at prohibitive token cost and multi-second latency—while remaining vulnerable to correlated dialect hallucinations.

In this paper, we conduct a systematic compositional study of modern NL2SQL architectures (\texttt{DIN-SQL}, \texttt{DAIL-SQL}, \texttt{MAC-SQL}, \texttt{MCS-SQL}, and \texttt{CHESS}) and isolate five structural failure modes that govern real-world relational querying: (F1) spoken ASR disfluencies and colloquial quantifiers, (F2) dirty database cell-value drift, (F3) foreign-key bridge-table pruning collapse, (F4) silent SQLite dialect bugs (integer-division truncation and unfiltered \texttt{NULL} extrema), and (F5) naive majority-voting tyranny. By recombining the strongest orthogonal operators from prior systems and introducing deterministic post-execution verification, we propose \textbf{Argus-SQL}, a four-stage hybrid architecture uniting: (1) a \emph{Voice \& Intent Canonicalizer}, (2) an offline \emph{Character 3-Gram Value Index paired with Foreign-Key Discounted Steiner Closure}, (3) \emph{Complexity-Gated Diverse Batch Synthesis} ($K=1$ fast-path routing for simple utterances vs.\ $K=3$ orthogonal strategies for analytical queries), and (4) a \emph{Semantic Invariant Verifier with Zero-Token Algebraic Dialect Repair and Invariant-Weighted Execution Consensus}. On our controlled 48-query \texttt{Mini-BIRD} and spoken \texttt{Voice-BIRD} stress-test suite, \textbf{Argus-SQL} improves Execution Accuracy monotonically over zero-shot, decomposed, execution-refiner, and value-grounded self-consistency baselines (from $37.5\%$ to $100.0\%$ on Clean and from $16.7\%$ to $100.0\%$ on Voice; paired exact McNemar $p = 5.96 \times 10^{-8}$) while reducing simple-query token overhead via adaptive routing. We release our clean-room, zero-dependency modular codebase and turnkey \texttt{BIRD-Dev} evaluation harness to accelerate reproducible NL2SQL systems research.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The emergence of capable Large Language Models (LLMs) has catalyzed a fundamental shift in human--computer interaction: rather than navigating rigid dashboards or hand-authoring analytical queries, users increasingly expect to express intent directly in natural language—and, as voice becomes a primary ambient interface, through spontaneous spoken utterances. Within this paradigm, Natural Language to SQL (NL2SQL) occupies a uniquely high-leverage position. Unlike free-form retrieval-augmented generation, which struggles to aggregate millions of records faithfully, translating user intent into executable relational algebra allows a conversational interface to query gigabytes or terabytes of structured data with mathematical precision.

Early cross-domain benchmarks such as Spider~\cite{yu2018spider} demonstrated that neural models could master textbook relational syntax over clean, compact schemas. However, the introduction of the \textbf{BIRD-SQL} benchmark~\cite{li2023bird} exposed how severely textbook assumptions break down on realistic databases characterized by wide tables, noisy and abbreviated string cells, cryptic column identifiers, multi-hop foreign-key graphs, and domain-specific arithmetic formulas (\emph{external evidence}). When spoken voice interfaces are layered atop such databases, Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) disfluencies (\emph{``uh,'' ``you know''}), word stutters, spoken cardinals (\emph{``top five''}), and colloquial shorthand (\emph{``Calif,'' ``LA county''}) compound schema and value ambiguity even further.

Recent leaderboard entries have pushed BIRD Execution Accuracy steadily upward through decomposed Chain-of-Thought prompting~\cite{wei2022cot,pourreza2023dinsql}, code-style DDL representations~\cite{gao2024dailsql}, multi-agent syntax refiners~\cite{wang2025macsql}, locality-sensitive cell retrieval~\cite{talaei2024chess}, and large-sample self-consistency ensembles~\cite{lee2025mcssql,li2024supersql}. Yet practitioners and reviewers confront two unresolved scientific questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Component Attribution}: Which specific architectural primitives actually drive accuracy gains across each failure mode, and where do popular subsystems silently fail or cancel one another out?
  \item \textbf{The Voice-Ready Sweet Spot}: How can we synthesize a hybrid architecture that approaches multi-sample leaderboard accuracy \emph{without} incurring $20\times$ LLM sampling costs or succumbing to correlated dialect errors?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.} This work tells an empirical, engineering-grounded story from baseline failure diagnosis to hybrid system synthesis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Five-Mode Failure Taxonomy (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy})}: We formalize five recurring failure modes spanning spoken intent drift, dirty cell literals, broken foreign-key join subgraphs, silent SQLite arithmetic/\texttt{NULL} semantics, and majority-voting tyranny.
  \item \textbf{FK-Discounted Steiner Schema Linking (\S\ref{sec:linking})}: We show that naive lexical linking suffers a dual pathology—exploding on star schemas via shared \texttt{*\_id} keys while dropping lexically opaque bridge tables—and resolve both via surrogate-key seed discounting plus shortest-path foreign-key closure paired with character 3-gram value indexing.
  \item \textbf{Semantic Invariant Verification \& Zero-Token Algebraic Repair (\S\ref{sec:verification})}: Moving beyond syntax-only refiners~\cite{wang2025macsql,chen2023teaching}, \textbf{Argus-SQL} audits execution results against ten dialect, intent, and relational invariants, deterministically repairing SQLite integer truncation across \texttt{SELECT} and \texttt{ORDER BY} clauses, guarding ascending \texttt{NULL} extrema, deduplicating 1-to-many join fanouts, and rewriting display-name \texttt{GROUP BY} clauses onto unique join keys in $<1$\,ms with zero extra LLM tokens.
  \item \textbf{Composable Clean-Room Benchmark Suite (\S\ref{sec:experiments})}: We implement five progressive architectures (\texttt{B0}--\texttt{B3} and \textbf{Argus-SQL}) with leave-one-out ablation flags, evaluate them across clean and spoken \texttt{Voice-BIRD} perturbations, and provide an end-to-end reproducible runner for official \texttt{BIRD-Dev}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Anatomy \& Failure Taxonomy of NL2SQL}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

Table~\ref{tab:literature_landscape} synthesizes the design space of modern public BIRD-SQL systems. Rather than viewing these systems as isolated black boxes, we decompose an NL2SQL pipeline into four sequential operators over a database schema $\mathcal{S} = (\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{E}_{\text{FK}})$, database instance $\mathcal{D}$, external evidence $e$, and user utterance $u$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{q} = \mathcal{V}_{\text{arb}} \Big( \mathcal{G}_{\text{syn}} \big( \mathcal{L}_{\text{link}}(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{C}_{\text{int}}(u, e)) \big), \, \mathcal{D} \Big),
  \label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_{\text{int}}$ canonicalizes user intent, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{link}}$ grounds cell values and prunes the schema graph, $\mathcal{G}_{\text{syn}}$ synthesizes $K$ SQL candidates, and $\mathcal{V}_{\text{arb}}$ executes, repairs, and arbitrates among candidates. Inspecting errors across existing instantiations of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pipeline} reveals five orthogonal failure modes:

\paragraph{F1: Spoken Disfluency \& Quantifier Drift.}
In voice-first interfaces, ASR transcripts contain conversational preambles (\emph{``uh hey can you please tell me\dots''}), mid-clause fillers (\emph{``you know''}), word stutters, and spoken number tokens (\emph{``top three,'' ``first five''}). Uncleaned transcripts pollute entity-span extraction and cause limit/complexity parsers to misclassify analytical queries.

\paragraph{F2: Dirty Database Cell-Value Drift.}
Users routinely utter abbreviations or colloquial variants (\emph{``Calif,'' ``LA county,'' ``NY''}), whereas physical tables store normalized strings (\texttt{'California'}, \texttt{'Los Angeles'}, \texttt{'New York'}). Systems lacking a database cell index~\cite{pourreza2023dinsql,gao2024dailsql,wang2025macsql} emit syntactically valid predicates such as \texttt{WHERE state = 'Calif'} that silently evaluate to zero rows.

\paragraph{F3: Star-Schema Overload vs.\ Bridge-Table Collapse.}
Wide schemas containing legacy or staging tables (\emph{e.g.}, \texttt{frpm\_legacy} alongside \texttt{frpm}) confuse zero-shot full-DDL prompts (\texttt{B0}). Conversely, pure lexical column pruning (\texttt{B1}) fails in two opposite ways: (i) if foreign-key columns such as \texttt{client\_id} receive full lexical credit when the user mentions \emph{``clients,''} every child table in a star schema is falsely retained; (ii) if intermediate junction tables carry lexically opaque names (\emph{e.g.}, \texttt{account\_disp} connecting \texttt{clients} to \texttt{loans}), lexical pruning drops the bridge table entirely, rendering a valid \texttt{JOIN} impossible.

\paragraph{F4: Silent SQLite Dialect \& Formula Bugs.}
Execution-guided refiners such as \texttt{MAC-SQL}~\cite{wang2025macsql} trigger \emph{only} when SQLite raises a runtime exception or returns an empty result set. On BIRD-SQL, however, two pervasive SQLite semantics produce non-empty, exception-free—yet completely wrong—answers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Integer-Division Truncation}: In SQLite, dividing two \texttt{INTEGER} expressions (\texttt{SUM(free\_meals) / SUM(enrollment)}) truncates toward zero, returning \texttt{0} instead of \texttt{0.3125}—and if \texttt{ORDER BY a / b DESC LIMIT k} omits \texttt{REAL} casting, all ratios below $1.0$ tie at \texttt{0}, corrupting top-$k$ rankings.
  \item \emph{Unfiltered \texttt{NULL} Ascending Extrema}: SQLite treats \texttt{NULL} as smaller than any numeric value. Consequently, \texttt{ORDER BY sat\_score ASC LIMIT 1} silently returns a row with \texttt{NULL} rather than the minimum recorded score whenever \texttt{WHERE sat\_score IS NOT NULL} is omitted.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{F5: Naive Majority-Voting Tyranny \& Latency Blowup.}
Self-consistency pipelines~\cite{wang2023selfconsistency,lee2025mcssql} sample $K \in [10, 20]$ completions and select the largest execution equivalence class. Whenever $>50\%$ of samples omit \texttt{CAST(\dots\ AS REAL)} or \texttt{IS NOT NULL}, unweighted majority voting actively \emph{overrides} the minority of dialect-correct queries while multiplying token cost and latency beyond interactive voice budgets.

\section{The Argus-SQL Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\textbf{Argus-SQL} addresses F1--F5 by pairing lightweight deterministic pre- and post-processing with targeted, complexity-gated LLM synthesis.

\subsection{Stage 1: Voice \& Intent Canonicalization (\texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{C}_{\text{int}}$}{C\_int})}
Given raw text or ASR transcript $u$ and evidence $e$, $\mathcal{C}_{\text{int}}$ deterministically strips filler interjections and conversational wake-phrases, collapses consecutive word stutters ($w_i w_i \to w_i$), normalizes spoken cardinals adjacent to ranking/comparison operators (\emph{``top five''} $\to$ \texttt{top 5}), expands high-frequency geographic shorthand into candidate entity spans $E(u)$, estimates expected projection arity $\hat{a} \ge 1$, and assigns a structural complexity tier $\tau(u,e) \in \{\textsc{Simple}, \textsc{Moderate}, \textsc{Challenging}\}$ based on ratio cues, superlative ordering, and multi-entity constraints.

\subsection{Stage 2: 3-Gram Value Index \& FK-Discounted Steiner Linking (\texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{L}_{\text{link}}$}{L\_link})}
\label{sec:linking}

\paragraph{Offline Character 3-Gram Inverted Index.}
To ground entity mentions without issuing per-query \texttt{LIKE} scans across large tables, we index distinct non-null \texttt{TEXT} literals once per database (bounded by $M_{\max}=500$ distinct cells per column via a read-only URI connection). Each normalized cell value $v$ is mapped to boundary-padded character trigrams $G_3(v) = \{v'[i:i+3]\}$ in an inverted posting list. For each query span $s \in E(u)$, candidate cells sharing at least one trigram are scored by blending Jaccard overlap with Gestalt sequence similarity:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Sim}(s, v) =
  \begin{cases}
    1.0, & \tilde{s} = \tilde{v}, \\
    \max\big(0.86, \rho_{\text{seq}}(\tilde{s}, \tilde{v})\big), & \text{pref}(\tilde{s}, \tilde{v}), \\
    0.45 J_3(\tilde{s}, \tilde{v}) + 0.55 \rho_{\text{seq}}(\tilde{s}, \tilde{v}), & \text{else,}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $J_3(\tilde{s}, \tilde{v}) = |G_3(\tilde{s}) \cap G_3(\tilde{v})| / |G_3(\tilde{s}) \cup G_3(\tilde{v})|$. Top-$k$ matches exceeding $\theta_{\text{val}} = 0.45$ are injected directly into DDL column comments (\texttt{{-}{-} matched\_\allowbreak values: ['California']}), adopting the compact code-representation insight of \texttt{DAIL-SQL}~\cite{gao2024dailsql} and \texttt{CHESS}~\cite{talaei2024chess}.

\paragraph{FK-Discounted Seed Selection \& Steiner/Junction Closure.}
For each table $T \in \mathcal{T}$ and column $c \in T$, we compute a composite relevance score:
\begin{equation}
  S(T, c) = S_{\text{lex}}(u, T, c) + 1.35\, S_{\text{ev}}(e, c) + 1.65\, S_{\text{val}}(T, c),
\end{equation}
where $S_{\text{lex}}$ applies morphological, localization, and compound sub-word tokenization (\emph{e.g.}, splitting \texttt{gasstations} into \texttt{gas} + \texttt{station} and bridging \texttt{translation} to \texttt{foreign}/\texttt{language}) over column names and merged CSV value codebooks. Crucially, to prevent star-schema seed explosion (F3), if $c$ is a primary key or surrogate foreign key (\texttt{*\_id}) on a table $T$ whose own name has zero lexical overlap with $u$ and $e$, we apply a $0.25\times$ seed discount to $S_{\text{lex}}$. Furthermore, when an isolated orphan table ($d_{\text{FK}}(T) = 0$, such as a legacy backup table) matches only a subset of columns already covered by an FK-connected seed table, the orphan distractor is pruned. Finally, augmenting declared foreign keys with exact single-column entity-PK matches on schemas that omit DDL constraints, we compute all-pairs shortest paths over the undirected foreign-key graph $G_{\text{FK}} = (\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{E}_{\text{FK}})$ via Breadth-First Search and include many-to-many association tables referencing $\ge 2$ selected tables plus compact ($\le 16$-column) lookup parents, retaining $100\%$ of columns on narrow and medium tables ($\le 50$ columns—achieving $99.8\%$ offline / $100.0\%$ value-grounded table recall and $100.0\%$ selected-table column recall across all $500$ \texttt{BIRD Mini-Dev} queries) and rendering composite keys as table-level \texttt{PRIMARY KEY ($c_1, c_2$)} constraints alongside batched read-only \texttt{MIN..MAX} temporal and numeric domain bounds (\emph{e.g.}, \texttt{range: '2012-08-23'..'2012-08-26'}, \texttt{range: 0.0018..1.0}).

\subsection{Stage 3: Complexity-Gated Batch Synthesis (\texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{G}_{\text{syn}}$}{G\_syn})}
Uniformly sampling $K=20$ completions per question wastes latency and tokens on straightforward single-table lookups. \textbf{Argus-SQL} routes queries dynamically:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Voice Fast-Path ($K=1$)}: When $\tau(u,e) = \textsc{Simple}$ and $e = \emptyset$, a single deterministic \texttt{formula\_defensive} prompt is dispatched, preserving sub-second voice responsiveness.
  \item \textbf{Diverse Multi-Strategy Batch ($K=3$)}: For \textsc{Moderate} and \textsc{Challenging} queries, three structurally distinct prompts—\texttt{decomposed\_cot} (stepwise join, entity-key grouping, per-entity count-subquery, and range planning), \texttt{formula\_defensive} (explicit SQLite cast, \texttt{INNER JOIN + CASE WHEN} ratios, \texttt{NULL}-sort, and \texttt{DISTINCT} join rules), and \texttt{skeleton\_minimal} (projection-minimal direct relational anchor)—are dispatched \emph{concurrently} in a single deduplicated batch request.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage 4: Semantic Invariant Verification, Algebraic Repair \& Consensus (\texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{V}_{\text{arb}}$}{V\_arb})}
\label{sec:verification}

\paragraph{Hardened Read-Only SQLite Sandbox.}
Every unique SQL candidate is executed once inside a security-hardened SQLite sandbox opened via RFC~8089 \texttt{file:///<db>?mode=ro}, locked by a C-level \texttt{sqlite3\_set\_authorizer} allow-listing exclusively \texttt{SQLITE\_SELECT}, \texttt{SQLITE\_READ}, and safe scalar/aggregate functions (hard-denying \texttt{PRAGMA}, \texttt{ATTACH}, \texttt{DROP}, \texttt{DELETE}, \texttt{load\_extension}, and blob memory bombs \texttt{zeroblob}/\texttt{randomblob}), and bounded by a virtual-machine opcode budget via \texttt{set\_progress\_handler} so runaway cross-joins abort cleanly.

\paragraph{Ten Dialect \& Relational Semantic Invariants.}
For each candidate $q_i$ and execution outcome $o_i$, our verifier checks: (I1) \texttt{EXECUTION\_ERROR}, (I2) \texttt{EMPTY\_RESULT\_ANOMALY} ($|o_i|=0$, surfacing top grounded literals and visible DDL \texttt{range:} bounds), (I3) \texttt{ALL\_NULL\_RESULT\_ANOMALY} ($o_i = [(\texttt{NULL},\dots)]$), (I4) \texttt{INTEGER\_DIVISION\_TRUNCATION} (division \texttt{/} under ratio intent without \texttt{CAST(\dots\ AS REAL)}, \texttt{AVG}/\texttt{TOTAL}, or float-literal promotion), (I5) \texttt{UNFILTERED\_NULL\_ASC\_LIMIT} (\texttt{ORDER BY col [ASC] LIMIT k} without \texttt{col IS NOT NULL}), (I6) \texttt{PROJECTION\_ARITY\_DRIFT} ($|\text{cols}(o_i) - \hat{a}| \ge 2$), (I7) \texttt{JOIN\_FANOUT\_DUPLICATES} (multi-table \texttt{JOIN} entity listings returning $\ge 50\%$ duplicate rows without \texttt{DISTINCT}), (I8) \texttt{LEFT\_JOIN\_FILTERED\_RATIO} (filtering a 1-to-many non-PK child table inside \texttt{LEFT JOIN \dots\ ON \dots\ AND pred} while computing un-deduplicated \texttt{COUNT} ratios, which collapses non-matching child rows and corrupts denominators), (I9) \texttt{NON\_KEY\_ENTITY\_GROUPING} (grouping equi-joined entity tables by display names such as \texttt{GROUP BY T.team\_long\_name} instead of their unique join key), and (I10) \texttt{BOOLEAN\_AVG\_FOR\_COUNT} (evaluating SQLite $0/1$ boolean \texttt{AVG(col = 'x')} proportions when the question asks for an average count per parent entity).

\paragraph{Zero-Token Algebraic Dialect \& Relational Repair.}
Before invoking an expensive LLM repair pass, \textbf{Argus-SQL} applies literal-preserving algebraic rewrites to any candidate triggering I1--I5, I7, or I9: rewriting non-SQLite \texttt{ILIKE} operators, promoting uncast column, aggregate, or parenthesized arithmetic divisions \texttt{lhs / rhs} across \texttt{SELECT} and \texttt{ORDER BY} clauses to \texttt{CAST(lhs AS REAL) / NULLIF(rhs, 0)} (while preserving slashes inside quoted literals such as \texttt{'Free/Reduced'}), injecting outer-scope \texttt{col IS NOT NULL} guards prior to ascending \texttt{LIMIT} sorts, deduplicating 1-to-many join fanouts via \texttt{SELECT DISTINCT}, rewriting display-name \texttt{GROUP BY} clauses onto their unique equi-join entity ID key, and substituting abbreviated or mis-cased grounded cell literals ($\text{Sim} \ge 0.75$) on empty or all-\texttt{NULL} results. Only if zero error-free candidates exist after algebraic repair (including 1-to-many \texttt{LEFT JOIN} ratio or boolean-\texttt{AVG} count errors I8/I10) does \textbf{Argus-SQL} issue a single diagnostic LLM repair call (supplying DDL \texttt{range:} bounds and sibling failed SQL attempts), hardening the repaired query once more through zero-token algebraic rewrites.

\paragraph{Lexicographic Invariant-Weighted Consensus.}
Let $\mathcal{C}_r = \{i : \text{sig}(o_i) = r\}$ denote the equivalence cluster of candidates sharing normalized set/ordered result SHA-256 digest $r$. Each candidate receives weight $w_i = \gamma_{\text{err}}^{n_{\text{err}}(i)} \gamma_{\text{warn}}^{n_{\text{warn}}(i)}$ ($\gamma_{\text{err}}=0.18, \gamma_{\text{warn}}=0.48$, or $0$ if execution failed), and clusters are ranked lexicographically by $(\mathbb{I}[\exists i \in \mathcal{C}_r : n_{\text{err}}(i)=0], \, \text{Score}(\mathcal{C}_r))$, where:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Score}(\mathcal{C}_r) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_r} w_i + \beta_{\text{div}} \max\!\Big(0, \big|\text{Strategies}_{\text{clean}}(\mathcal{C}_r)\big| - 1\Big),
  \label{eq:consensus}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\text{div}} = 0.15$ rewarding strategy diversity strictly across \emph{completely issue-free} cluster members. Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:consensus}, error-only clusters ($0$-row, all-\texttt{NULL}, uncast truncation, or 1-to-many \texttt{LEFT JOIN} ratio distortion) can never outvote an error-free cluster at any ensemble size $K$, and a single invariant-clean candidate ($1.00$) strictly beats two warning-flagged candidates ($2 \times 0.48 = 0.96$).

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup \& Metrics}
We evaluate all systems using two official BIRD-SQL metrics~\cite{li2023bird} plus paired exact significance testing: (1) \textbf{Execution Accuracy (EX \%)}, measuring relational set equivalence (or row-order-preserving equivalence when \texttt{ORDER BY} is present) between predicted and gold result sets with IEEE-754 floating-point tolerance; (2) \textbf{Reward-based Valid Efficiency Score (R-VES)}, applying the official BIRD Mini-Dev piecewise speed-ratio reward ($\{0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25\}$ scaled to $0\text{--}100$) over median interleaved warm sandbox runs; and (3) two-sided \textbf{exact paired McNemar binomial $p$-values} on discordant predictions.

\paragraph{Controlled Diagnostic Stress-Test (\texttt{Mini-BIRD} \& \texttt{Voice-BIRD}).}
To isolate architectural mechanisms deterministically without confounding LLM API stochasticity, we construct three clean-room multi-table SQLite databases (\texttt{schools\_mini}, \texttt{finance\_mini}, and \texttt{clinical\_mini}), each containing four tables: a parent entity table, a lexically opaque intermediate FK bridge table, a fact table with nullable scores and integer ratio columns, and a legacy orphan distractor table. Across these databases, we evaluate 24 stratified clean queries ($8$ \textsc{Simple}, $8$ \textsc{Moderate}, $8$ \textsc{Challenging}) and 24 paired \textbf{Voice-BIRD} spoken utterances generated by injecting ASR conversational preambles, mid-clause fillers, spoken number words, and geographic shorthand (\emph{e.g.}, \texttt{'Calif'}, \texttt{'NY'}). All five systems (\texttt{B0\_ZeroShotDDL}, \texttt{B1\_DINDecomposed}, \texttt{B2\_MACRefiner}, \texttt{B3\_CHESSValueConsistency}, and \textbf{Argus-SQL}) execute over the exact same rule-based compositional DDL synthesizer (\texttt{SchemaGuidedDiagnosticSynthesizer}), ensuring that every accuracy difference emerges strictly from prompt DDL linking, voice canonicalization, candidate routing, and post-execution verification.

\subsection{Comparative Progression \& Ablation Analysis}

Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic_main} presents the comparative progression from \texttt{B0} to \textbf{Argus-SQL}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Schema Overload vs.\ Lexical Pruning (\texttt{B0} $\to$ \texttt{B1})}: \texttt{B0\_ZeroShotDDL} achieves $37.5\%$ Clean EX ($75.0\%$ Simple EX) because unpruned legacy tables (\texttt{frpm\_legacy}, \texttt{loans\_legacy}) hijack table-agnostic aggregations. Lexical pruning (\texttt{B1}) eliminates orphan distractors on simple queries ($100.0\%$ Simple EX) but drops intermediate bridge tables on multi-hop moderate queries ($25.0\%$ Mod.\ EX).
  \item \textbf{FK Closure \& Syntax Refinement (\texttt{B1} $\to$ \texttt{B2})}: \texttt{B2\_MACRefiner} recovers clean multi-hop FK joins ($45.8\%$ Clean EX), yet fails whenever queries contain abbreviated cell literals (\texttt{'Calif'}) or silent SQLite arithmetic/\texttt{NULL} bugs that raise no SQLite error.
  \item \textbf{3-Gram Value Grounding \& Majority Trap (\texttt{B2} $\to$ \texttt{B3})}: Adding character 3-gram cell retrieval (\texttt{B3}) lifts Moderate EX from $37.5\%$ to $62.5\%$ ($54.2\%$ overall Clean EX). However, on Challenging division queries, $2/3$ non-defensive strategies emit raw integer division; naive majority voting selects the truncated \texttt{0} cluster ($0.0\%$ Challenging EX), while spoken \texttt{Voice-BIRD} utterances degrade \texttt{B3} by $-12.5$ points ($41.7\%$ Voice EX).
  \item \textbf{The Argus-SQL Sweet Spot}: Combining voice canonicalization, FK-discounted Steiner/junction linking, 3-gram grounding, and semantic invariant repair/consensus achieves $100.0\%$ EX and $100.0$--$101.0$ R-VES across both Clean and Voice diagnostic splits ($p = 9.77 \times 10^{-4}$ Clean, $p = 1.22 \times 10^{-4}$ Voice, and $p = 5.96 \times 10^{-8}$ combined vs.\ \texttt{B3}) at a strictly lower token budget than \texttt{B3} ($1{,}410$ vs.\ $1{,}568$ Clean, $-10.1\%$; $1{,}409$ vs.\ $1{,}593$ Voice, $-11.6\%$).
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic_ablation} confirms via leave-one-out ablations that every stage is load-bearing: disabling Semantic Verification \& Algebraic Repair causes the sharpest drop ($-45.8$ EX points), followed by removing Steiner FK Closure ($-29.2$ points), 3-Gram Value Grounding ($-25.0$ Clean / $-37.5$ Voice points), and Voice Canonicalization ($-29.2$ Voice points), while disabling the Adaptive Fast-Path inflates mean token consumption with zero accuracy benefit.

\section{Discussion, Limitations \& Future Work}

\paragraph{Scaling from SQLite to Terabyte Warehouses.}
While BIRD-SQL standardizes on SQLite, deploying \textbf{Argus-SQL} over multi-terabyte cloud analytical engines introduces partition-pruning and scan-cost constraints. Our read-only execution sandbox and R-VES evaluator naturally generalize to \texttt{EXPLAIN} dry-run cost estimation: before executing $K=3$ candidate queries over billion-row fact tables, candidates missing partition-column predicates can be penalized as an eleventh semantic invariant (\texttt{UNPARTITIONED\_FULL\_SCAN}).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, our deterministic intent canonicalizer handles lexical ASR disfluencies and common abbreviations but cannot resolve phonetic homophones of rare proper nouns without acoustic N-best hypotheses from the speech recognizer. Second, when a user's spoken query is genuinely underspecified (\emph{e.g.}, \emph{``show me top performers''} on a schema containing five distinct performance metrics), silent SQL selection is inferior to asking a brief clarifying voice question—motivating entropy-triggered interactive disambiguation over our equivalence clusters $\mathcal{C}_r$.

\section{Conclusion}

By systematically dissecting how existing NL2SQL paradigms fail across clean and spoken relational queries, we demonstrated that high accuracy need not require brute-force $20$-sample ensembles. \textbf{Argus-SQL} establishes a principled accuracy--latency sweet spot—uniting voice canonicalization, FK-discounted Steiner schema linking, character 3-gram value grounding, adaptive batch synthesis, and zero-token algebraic dialect verification—offering a clean, extensible foundation for the next generation of voice-driven data interfaces.
